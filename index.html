The one-question test

Do you have it?

Think about your mom. There are four ways people do it. Read all four, then answer one question.

  1. 1 · A picture of her. It has shape, size and color, and it feels like looking at something.
  2. 2 · Her voice. You hear her voice in your head.
  3. 3 · Just knowing. You just know you’re thinking about her. To feel this one, stare at something near you and pay attention to its color. You still know you’re thinking about your mom.
  4. No shape.
    No size.
    No color.
    4 · A concept bubble of her. It feels like looking at something, but it has no shape, size or color, and it’s obviously your mom.

Can you do the fourth one?

The sense

What is thoughtfeel?

thoughtfeel is a sense for concepts. It lets you take in a concept and its properties all at once, the way vision lets you take in what’s in front of you.

You could describe a dog in writing: it has fur, a wet black nose, and it’s about the size of a capybara. Or you could look at it, and vision gives you all of that at once, with no list.

Concepts have properties too, including properties you could never see. Take the economy. You could write out what’s true of it: millions of people, making and trading things, sometimes booming and sometimes in a slump. People with thoughtfeel can still make that list in words. But when they use thoughtfeel, the sense takes in properties like these all at once.

That makes comparing fast. Show someone photos of two dogs, and they can tell right away whether it’s the same dog. If it isn’t, they can look again and point to why: one has a brown tail, the other a white one. With thoughtfeel, the same works for concepts you imagine. If two concepts aren’t the same, that’s clear right away, and paying attention to each one shows which properties differ.

Like vision and hearing, it differs from person to person, and it most likely varies the way the mind’s eye does: most people have some of it, some have none, and among those who have it, it ranges from faint to vivid. For some people, taking in and imagining concepts is the default way they experience the world. For others, it’s much quieter. Just as people can imagine in pictures or in sounds, people with thoughtfeel can imagine in concepts.

Is it really all at once?

Taking in the concept is. Putting its properties into words comes after. When a friend walks into a room, you know it’s them right away, before you could describe a single feature. If someone asks what color their eyes are, you look again and say. thoughtfeel works the same way. Someone with thoughtfeel who thinks of a promise knows right away what it is. Then they can pay attention to it and put its properties into words: it’s made to someone, it’s about the future, and it can be broken.

With practice, people notice finer properties, the way a birdwatcher learns to spot details in a bird that look the same to everyone else.

Why thoughtfeel works: follow a conceptual story the way your mind’s eye helps you follow a fairy tale

If I tell you, “A boy and a girl went to the well. The boy pumped water, and water came out of the well. The girl took a bucket and poured the water on a patch of green grass,” you can understand and remember that story better by using your mind’s eye to imagine it.

But if I tell you, “The mother of the regent of the land is intrigued by the prospect of discovery,” you can’t use your mind’s eye to follow along, because the story is conceptual, not visual.

With thoughtfeel, though, you can use your ability to imagine in concepts to build that story in your imagination. That helps you understand the conceptual story the same way your mind’s eye helps you understand the visual one.

thoughtfeel also helps with mental simulation of concepts, the same way that your mind’s eye helps with mental simulation of objects. If you want to figure out what happens when you turn a gear in a gearbox, you can use your mind’s eye to run the simulation. In thoughtfeel, when you’ve built a conceptual machine like an algorithm or a biological process, you can simulate how the system would behave if you changed a conceptual property.

The lessons

What is it useful for?

thoughtfeel is a sense, like your mind’s eye or your inner voice. Those senses come with well-known techniques you can learn to remember and learn better. The memory palace uses your mind’s eye to pull off remarkable feats of memory, like memorizing a 1,000-digit number in an hour. Your inner voice remembers more when you put the words to a song, which is why the alphabet song works.

thoughtfeel has techniques of its own, and learning them can improve how you think and learn. Start with these two videos.

About the author

Hi, I’m Ted Shachtman

I’m a teacher and software engineer with a deep interest in cognitive science. I have degrees in elementary education and cognitive studies from Vanderbilt University, and I’m finishing a master’s in computer science at the University of Colorado Boulder in November 2026.
