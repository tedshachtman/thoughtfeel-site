What are concept bubbles?

Concept bubbles is the application of unsymbolized thinking to learning. A concept bubble is one idea held in unsymbolized thinking, with no words and no picture. Learners hold each idea or flashcard as a concept bubble and link it to the one before.

It uses unsymbolized thinking, the psychologist Russell Hurlburt’s name for a thought that comes without words or pictures.

The technique

Follow a conceptual story the way your mind’s eye follows a fairy tale

If I tell you, “A boy and a girl went to the well. The boy pumped water, and water came out of the well. The girl took a bucket and poured the water on a patch of green grass,” you can understand and remember that story better by using your mind’s eye to imagine it.

But if I tell you, “The mother of the regent of the land is intrigued by the prospect of discovery,” you can’t use your mind’s eye to follow along, because the story is conceptual, not visual.

With concept bubbles, though, you can use your ability to imagine in concepts to build that story in your imagination. That helps you understand the conceptual story the same way your mind’s eye helps you understand the visual one.

The bubble

Like vision, for concepts

You could describe a dog in writing: it has fur, a wet black nose, and it’s about the size of a capybara. Or you could look at it, and vision gives you all of that at once, with no list.

Concepts have properties too, including properties you could never see. Take the economy. You could write out what’s true of it: millions of people, making and trading things, sometimes booming and sometimes in a slump. People who use concept bubbles can still make that list in words. But in a concept bubble, properties like these are taken in all at once.

That makes comparing fast. Show someone photos of two dogs, and they can tell right away whether it’s the same dog. If it isn’t, they can look again and point to why: one has a brown tail, the other a white one. With concept bubbles, the same works for concepts you imagine. If two concepts aren’t the same, that’s clear right away, and paying attention to each one shows which properties differ.

Is it really all at once?

Taking in the concept is. Putting its properties into words comes after. When a friend walks into a room, you know it’s them right away, before you could describe a single feature. If someone asks what color their eyes are, you look again and say. A concept bubble works the same way. Someone who holds a promise as a concept bubble knows right away what it is. Then they can pay attention to it and put its properties into words: it’s made to someone, it’s about the future, and it can be broken.

With practice, people notice finer properties, the way a birdwatcher learns to spot details in a bird that look the same to everyone else.

Who can do it

Can everyone do it?

Concept bubbles use unsymbolized thinking, and not everyone has it. Like vision and hearing, it differs from person to person, and it most likely varies the way the mind’s eye does. In the best-known study, 22 of 30 college students had at least one moment of it in about ten random samples each; some had it in none of their moments and one in 80% of them. There is no figure yet for how many people in general have it. For some people, taking in and imagining concepts is the default way they experience the world. For others, it’s much quieter.

Source: Russell Hurlburt’s study of inner experience (Heavey & Hurlburt, 2008). 22 of 30 people had unsymbolized thinking at least once: a specific thought that comes without words or pictures. Hurlburt named and studies unsymbolized thinking. The concept bubbles technique is Ted Shachtman’s, and Hurlburt isn’t involved in it.

What it isn’t

What a concept bubble is not

  • Not a diagram or a mind map. Nothing is drawn. A concept bubble is held in your mind. The lessons show bubbles on screen only to point at which concept is meant.
  • Not your mind’s eye or inner voice. Pictures and words can come along with a thought, but a concept bubble works without them.
  • Not an emotion. The economy and justice feel different from each other, but neither one feels happy or sad.
  • Not the feeling of knowing. Everyone knows what they’re thinking. A concept bubble gives the thought itself detail you can notice, the way a picture has detail.
  • Not the same thing as unsymbolized thinking. Unsymbolized thinking is the kind of thought. Concept bubbles is the technique that uses it on purpose, the way the memory palace uses the mind’s eye.

The lessons

What is it useful for?

Your mind’s eye and your inner voice come with well-known techniques you can learn to remember and learn better. The memory palace uses your mind’s eye to pull off remarkable feats of memory, like memorizing a 1,000-digit number in an hour. Your inner voice remembers more when you put the words to a song, which is why the alphabet song works.

The name

It used to be called thoughtfeel

From September to November 2026 the technique, and the kind of thinking it uses, were both called thoughtfeel. The videos and posts from then still use that name.

Since November 2026 the technique is called concept bubbles, and the kind of thinking goes by its research name, unsymbolized thinking. thoughtfeel is still the name of this site.
